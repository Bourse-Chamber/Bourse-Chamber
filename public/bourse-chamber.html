The bench

Nine doctrines that disagree on purpose.

01

Benjamin Graham

Margin of safety

Asks what the token is still worth when the story stops working. No margin, no support — at any size.

02

Charlie Munger

Mental models

Runs the inversion checklist: not how this wins, but every ordinary way it fails. Kills hype before it reaches the floor.

03

Peter Lynch

Know what you own

Demands the protocol be explainable in one plain sentence. If it takes a whitepaper, it takes a pass.

04

Cathie Wood

Growth and disruption

Maps adoption curves and network effects, arguing the long arc while the rest of the table stares at the last candle.

05

Aswath Damodaran

Story into numbers

Turns every narrative into cash flows, growth rates and a range. A price without a model is just a mood.

06

Nassim Nicholas Taleb

Tail risk

Sizes the position for the worst week, not the best month. Barbell first, conviction second.

07

Mohnish Pabrai

Low risk, high uncertainty

Hunts setups where the downside is capped and the outcome stays wide open — and clones the best argument in the room.

08

Bill Ackman

Concentrated conviction

Backs only a thesis he would defend in public against the other eight. Diversification is not an argument.

09

Michael Burry

Contrarian audit

Reads the leverage nobody quotes and the euphoria everybody feels. The seat that says no while the chart says yes.

These are AI reasoning personas built from each investor's publicly documented philosophy. They are not the individuals, they do not speak for them, and nothing here is financial advice.
